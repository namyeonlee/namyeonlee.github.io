“You teach me, I forget. You show me, I remember. You involve me, I understand.” — E.O. Wilson

This quote resonates with how I approach teaching: students learn best when they are actively involved. My classes move beyond lectures into real-world projects, client collaborations, and professional simulations. Whether students are role-playing a crisis scenario, developing a PR campaign for a local nonprofit, or experimenting with AI tools in research, they learn by doing—building adaptability, creativity, and confidence.
I continually update my courses with current industry practices and emerging technologies so students graduate ready for today’s fast-changing communication and media landscape.

Current (FL 25)

Introduction to Public Relations (PRE 2200)
Foundations, ethics, and trends in public relations
  • Covers PR history, principles, theories, and career paths
  • Activities: news briefings, crisis simulations, career workshops
  • Group projects: PR in the News and International PR campaigns
Social Media for Public Relations (PRE 3600)
Strategic social media in PR, advertising, and marketing
  • Combines academic theory, case studies, and campaign design
  • Semester-long group project: brand analysis, social media audit, strategy creation, and sample content development
  • Trend analysis and influencer marketing discussions
Mass Communication Theory and Research (MCM 4360)
Exploring major media theories and research methods
  • Covers key theories such as agenda-setting, framing, and diffusion of innovations
  • Teaches both quantitative and qualitative methods, with attention to ethics in research
  • Includes hands-on projects, AI simulations, and a theory mapping assignment connecting concepts to real-world media issues
